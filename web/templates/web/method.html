How NextTrack chooses

NextTrack compares songs on four things it can measure without listening data: genre tags, mood, tempo and release decade. It blends them with weights you can change, then makes sure the list is not ten versions of the same song.

The four comparisons

Genre

Each song carries weighted genre tags. Tags are expanded up a small family tree, so "indie rock" also counts a little towards "rock". Rare tags get more weight than common ones (inverse document frequency), then two songs are compared with cosine similarity.

cos(idf-weighted tag vectors)

Mood

Moods are points on a valence (unhappy to happy) and arousal (calm to intense) plane. Nearby moods score high, so euphoric is close to happy and far from melancholic.

exp(-d² / 2σ²), σ = 0.75

Tempo

Tempo is compared as a ratio, because 60 vs 70 BPM is a far bigger change than 160 vs 170. A song at half or double the speed still counts as a groove match, with a penalty.

exp(-d² / 2σ²), d = |log₂(a/b)|, σ = 0.12

Decade

Release years that are close together sound like they belong to the same era. Similarity fades smoothly with the gap instead of jumping at decade boundaries.

exp(-Δyears² / 2σ²), σ = 12

Blending

The score for a pair of songs is the weighted average of whichever comparisons are available for both. If a song has no tempo or year, that comparison is skipped and the remaining weights are rescaled, with a small penalty so complete matches win ties. New or sparsely tagged songs can still be recommended.

With several seed songs, the score is the average across seeds (Blend them) or the best single match (Closest to any).

Variety

A plain top-ten often returns an artist's whole album. NextTrack re-ranks with Maximal Marginal Relevance: each pick is scored by relevance minus similarity to what is already chosen, scaled by the Variety slider. A per-artist limit applies on top.

Likes and skips

When "Learn from my likes and skips" is on, liked songs join the seeds at half weight. Skipped songs disappear and songs that resemble them are pushed down.

Reasons

The coloured bar under each result shows how many points each feature contributed. The sentence beneath is generated from the same numbers: shared tags, mood distance, tempo gap and era.

Listening

NextTrack doesn't host or stream audio — it has no licence to, and neither Last.fm nor MusicBrainz provides playable tracks. The play icon next to a result opens a YouTube search for the title and artist in a new tab, so you're always one click from actually hearing it.

Where the data comes from

  • Demo catalogue: a hand-curated CSV of well-known songs with approximate BPM, year, mood and genre tags. Treat the values as illustrative.
  • Last.fm: genre tags, listener counts and similar-track lists (used as evaluation ground truth).
  • MusicBrainz: release years and identifiers.
  • Mood is inferred from tag words such as "chill" or "melancholy". Tempo is not provided by either service, so imported songs have none unless you add it.

Limits worth knowing

  • Content-based similarity finds songs that sound alike on paper. It cannot know that two very different songs share a fan base.
  • Tags follow what listeners write, so they lean towards Western, English-language music.
  • Mood is one label per song, which flattens songs that change character.
